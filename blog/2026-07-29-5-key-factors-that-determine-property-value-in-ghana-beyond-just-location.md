# Compensation Valuation Explained: What Landowners and Communities Should Know About Mining & Resettlement Projects in Ghana

For many families and communities in Ghana's mining regions, the arrival of a mining concession or a government-backed resettlement project raises one urgent question: "What is my land and property actually worth, and what am I entitled to?"

At Kwaadjei Valuation Hub, we've worked directly with mining companies, resettlement agencies, and affected communities to answer that question fairly and transparently. This post breaks down how compensation valuation actually works, so you know what to expect and how to protect your interests.

## What Is Compensation Valuation?

Compensation valuation is the professional process of determining the fair monetary value owed to a landowner, tenant, or community when their land, buildings, crops, or other assets are compulsorily acquired, disrupted, or displaced. Typically for mining operations, infrastructure projects, or government resettlement schemes.

Unlike a standard market valuation, compensation valuation must account for loss of livelihood, disturbance, and disruption, not just the physical value of land and structures.

## What Gets Valued?

A thorough compensation assessment typically covers:

* **Land value:** Based on the size, location, and use of the affected land
* **Buildings and structures:** Homes, stores, outbuildings, and other improvements
* **Crops and economic trees:** Especially critical in farming communities, where cocoa, oil palm, and other tree crops represent years of investment
* **Loss of business or livelihood:** For landowners whose income depends on the affected land
* **Disturbance allowance:** Compensation for the inconvenience and cost of relocating

Communities are often surprised to learn how many of these categories apply to them. A farmer displaced by a mining concession, for example, is entitled to far more than the bare cost of the land itself.

## How Is the Value Determined?

Professional valuers use recognized approaches, primarily the Comparative (Market) Method for land and the Cost/Replacement Method for buildings and improvements while factoring in guidelines set by the Lands Commission and, where applicable, international standards such as IFC Performance Standard 5 for resettlement.

Crop valuation typically follows established government or industry crop compensation rate schedules, adjusted for maturity, yield potential, and remaining productive life.

## Common Pitfalls Landowners Face

We frequently see the same issues arise in the field:

1. **Accepting the acquiring company's first offer without independent verification.** Compensation schedules from a mining company or developer should always be checked by an independent, accredited valuer.
2. **Incomplete documentation**. Landowners without clear title or registered interests often struggle to prove their claim which is why land documentation matters long before a project ever arrives (see our [guide to land title registration](https://kwaadjeivaluation.com/blog/land-title-registration-ghana/)).
3. **Overlooking disturbance and livelihood loss.** Many claims focus only on land and buildings, leaving significant entitlements unclaimed.
4. **Community-wide assessments without individual verification**. Blanket rates can undervalue specific households with above-average improvements or crop holdings.

## Why an Independent, Accredited Valuer Matters

Because compensation directly affects people's homes and livelihoods, the valuation must be independent, defensible, and compliant with Ghana Institution of Surveyors (GhIS) standards. An accredited valuer represents the landowner's interests with the same rigor a mining company's own valuer applies on the other side, ensuring the final figure reflects true, fair value rather than a negotiated shortcut.
